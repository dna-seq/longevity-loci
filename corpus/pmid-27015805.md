Pilling et al. 2016, Aging (Albany NY). GWAS of parents' age at death in 'white' British UK Biobank participants aged
55 to 70 (BOLT-LMM, imputed dosages), on three continuous phenotypes and one binary one, "extreme longevity": at
least one parent in the top 1 % of the age-at-death distribution (mothers 98 or older, fathers 95 or older). Read
from Europe PMC's open full text (PMC4833145), and Supplementary Table 2 (the 1,000 most significant variants of each
GWAS) and Supplementary Figures 8 to 9 from Europe PMC's supplementary archive.

## Reading 2026-11-11, full_text

> [q1] In addition we created a binary “extreme longevity” phenotype based on the top 1% of the age at death distribution for mothers and fathers (≥98 years and ≥95 years, respectively).

The binary phenotype: at least one parent in the top 1 % of age at death.

> [q2] therefore 1,339 participants had at least one parent (alive or dead) who lived to an “extreme age” (3.2% of 42,273 participants; those with ‘discordant’ parents – one long-lived and one short-lived – are excluded).

The binary GWAS's sample: 1,339 cases of 42,273 (the catalog's GCST003395 gives 1,339 cases and 40,934 controls).

> [q3] Our sample included ‘white’ British UK Biobank participants aged 55-70 years old

The ancestry: European, British.

> [q4] We used BOLT-LMM to model the associations between imputed variants (dosages) and each phenotype [41], which uses a linear mixed effects model approach.

The model: a linear mixed model, also for the binary phenotype, so its β is a difference in case probability per
allele, not a log odds ratio.

> [q5] In the GWAS against the binary “extreme age” phenotype (at least one parent very long-lived) two variants (rs528161076 and rs75824829, on chromosome 7 and 9 respectively; see Figures Supplementary 8-9 for Locus Zoom plots) were found to be significant (p<5×10−8).

rs528161076 passes 5e-8 in the binary GWAS.

> [q6] however rs528161076 is in a distinct peak of variants in an intron of AP5Z1 (adaptor related protein complex 5, zeta 1 subunit, thought to be involved in homologous recombination DNA double-strand break repair).

The authors place it in an AP5Z1 intron.

> [q7] The association between rs528161076 and extreme longevity should still be interpreted with caution, as the variant is not associated with the continuous age at death variables (combined parent's age at death p=0.65, father's p=0.47 and mother's p=0.96).

The same paper's continuous parental age-at-death GWAS, on the same people, finds nothing at the variant.

> [q8] We also identified a variant associated with extreme longevity, in the AP5Z1 gene locus, a gene encoding a homologous recombination DNA double-strand break repair helicase protein [22], although this association with longevity appears less robust.

> [q9] The GWAS analysis revealed two suggestive genetic variants that require further study;

The authors call it suggestive and unreplicated in their discussion, though its P passes 5e-8.

> [q10] For the AP5Z1 variant, this involves a C insertion after the T allele, with a prevalence 4.5% with at least one copy and is enriched amongst the those with top 1% longevity parents: of 1,335 participants with at least one long-lived parent, 84 have at least one of these insertions (6.3%) compared to 4.39% (1,796 of 40,871) of controls.

The direction: the C insertion is the longer-life allele, carried by 6.3 % of cases and 4.39 % of controls. The crude
carrier odds ratio from these counts is (84/1,251)/(1,796/39,075) = 1.46 (derived here).

> [q11] Finding big enough independent samples to confirm these associations will be challenging.

No replication was attempted.
