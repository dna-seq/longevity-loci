Rosoff et al. 2023, Nature Aging. A multivariate GWAS (genomic SEM) of one common factor, "mvAge", behind five
ageing GWAS: healthspan, frailty, exceptional longevity, parental lifespan and epigenetic age acceleration
(PhenoAge), effective n about 1.96 million Europeans; 52 lead SNPs in 38 loci, 20 of them novel (Table 1). Its
inputs reuse UK Biobank and LifeGen (Zenin 2019's healthspan, Timmers 2019's parental lifespan), so it replicates
none of them. Read from Europe PMC's open full text (PMC10432278); the supplementary tables (MOESM3) cached 2026-11-11.

> [q2] The univariate input GWASs of frailty and EAA were reverse coded to align their effects to have positive relationships with healthspan, lifespan and extreme longevity.

The orientation: frailty and epigenetic age acceleration were reverse coded so that every input points the same
way as healthspan, lifespan and longevity; a positive β on mvAge is the healthier-ageing side. G at rs3768321 is
on it, as it is in the component studies' own summary statistics (pmid-30729179#q14, pmid-30642433#q25).

> [q3] Lead SNPs were defined as novel if they were >1 Mb from previously identified loci in the univariate aging-related GWASs comprising the mvAge.

"Novel" means more than 1 Mb from every locus of the five input GWAS: none of them found rs3768321 genome-wide on
its own.

> [q4] Because the null hypothesis of the QSNP test is that the SNP associations on the univariate GWASs are statistically mediated by the resultant multivariate GWAS, significant QSNP tests in the multivariate GWAS summary statistics suggest that the SNP impacts the univariate GWASs by pathways other than mvAge

The Q test: a significant Q P says the SNP acts on the inputs by pathways other than the common factor. rs3768321's
0.02 is nominal, a hint that its effect is not spread evenly over the five traits.

> [q5] At least 571,260 unique genomes are represented in our analysis (Supplementary Table 1), accounting for maximum potential overlap between the study cohorts contributing to the five aging GWAS study cohorts included in the multivariate GWAS analysis, as well as the potential overlap of UKB and other non-UKB UK cohorts, and the overlap of genomes underlying the parental lifespans cohorts.

The inputs overlap: UK Biobank and the parental-lifespan cohorts underlie several of them.

> [q6] Therefore, resulting multivariate GWASs, including mvAge, do not have conventional units14.

mvAge has no conventional unit: its β is not years or a hazard.
