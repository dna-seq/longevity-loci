Liu et al. 2021, Aging Cell. A longevity case-control GWAS in the Chinese Longitudinal Healthy Longevity Survey
(CLHLS): dataset 1 (a custom chip of about 27,000 preselected SNPs, imputed) and dataset 2 (an earlier genome-wide
CLHLS sample, Zeng 2016), meta-analysed; Chinese only. Read from Europe PMC's open full text (PMC7963337). GWAS
Catalog GCST012210. The supplementary tables (S1 to S18) are listed by Europe PMC; its bundle downloads slowly
through the proxy and was not read for this reading, so Table S2's chip components and Table S16's survival results
are not read.

## Reading 2026-11-11, full_text

> [q1] Novel longevity loci, BMPER (rs17169634; p = 7.91 × 10−15) and TMEM43/XPC (rs1043943; p = 3.59 × 10−8), were identified in a case–control analysis of 11,045 individuals.

The abstract's claim: rs1043943 at TMEM43/XPC, genome-wide in the case-control meta-analysis of 11,045. For row 18.

> [q2] rs1043943 3 14183410 TMEM43 T C 0.165 0.033 4.24E‐07 0.081 0.042 0.016 0.146 0.029 3.59E‐08 Parkinson's disease (p = 0.045)

Table 1, rs1043943 (GRCh37 3:14,183,410), effect allele T, other C: dataset 1 β 0.165 (SE 0.033), P 4.24 × 10⁻⁷;
dataset 2 without the overlapping samples β 0.081 (SE 0.042), P 0.016; meta-analysis β 0.146 (SE 0.029), P 3.59 ×
10⁻⁸; Parkinson's disease P 0.045 in dataset 1. T, the minor allele, goes with longevity. The row does not agree
with itself: β/SE gives z 1.93 (P about 0.054) for dataset 2 and z 5.03 (P about 4.8 × 10⁻⁷) for the meta-analysis,
and an inverse-variance pooling of the two datasets' printed β and SE gives P about 3 × 10⁻⁷; the GWAS Catalog's CI
for the meta β (0.09 to 0.203, db-gwascatalog-2026.10.29#q132) implies the same SE 0.029; and Table 2's sex-specific
metas (q9) pooled the same way give β 0.147, SE 0.029, P about 3.5 × 10⁻⁷, reproducing the meta β and SE and missing
the printed P about tenfold. The paper's printed P is its own call (D17.3); the discrepancy is said, not corrected.
At about 4 × 10⁻⁷ the result would be suggestive. For row 18.

> [q3] After standard quality control process, 8,490 individuals (4,662 cases with an age ≥90 and 3,828 controls with an age <75; 75 is the average life span of Chinese individuals) in dataset 1 and 2,555 independent individuals (removing 1,922 samples overlapped between 8,490 individuals in dataset 1 and 4,477 individuals in dataset 2; remaining 1,105 centenarians and 1,450 controls with age <65) in dataset 2 were included for case–control association analysis.

The design: dataset 1, 4,662 cases aged 90 or more against 3,828 controls under 75; dataset 2, after removing the
overlap, 1,105 centenarians against 1,450 controls under 65. Cases above an age against younger living controls, not
a birth-cohort percentile; the paper states no percentile. For row 18.

> [q4] For replication purposes, a dataset 2 included 4477 individuals based on our previous study (Zeng et al., 2016). 2054 samples were overlapped between the two datasets.

> [q5] (1) 11,893 SNPs associated with longevity based on our previous CLHLS GWAS study on 4477 Chinese individuals (Zeng et al., 2016);

Dataset 2 is the earlier CLHLS GWAS of 4,477 (Zeng 2016), and 11,893 of dataset 1's 27,656 chip SNPs were chosen
from that same GWAS. Whether rs1043943 entered the chip that way is not read (Table S2 not read): if it did, dataset
2's P is part of the reason the SNP was tested at all. The overlap is 2,054 in the
methods and 1,922 in Table 1's footnote. For row 18.

> [q6] Therefore, we performed both sex‐combined and sex‐stratified meta‐analyses of these two independent datasets including 11,045 individuals to further identify potential longevity genes and pathways.

The paper calls the two datasets independent after removing the overlap; dataset 2 sits inside the meta-analysis
that reached P < 5 × 10⁻⁸, so it is part of the genome-wide result, not a replication of it. For row 18.

> [q7] The third top SNP, rs1043943 (p = 3.59 × 10−8), is located in the 3'‐UTR of TMEM43, which is in strong linkage disequilibrium (LD) with rs2228001 (r 2 = 0.95; p = 1.13 × 10−7), a missense mutation in the XPC gene.

The lead is in TMEM43's 3' UTR, in LD (r² 0.95) with rs2228001, a missense variant of XPC; the paper points to XPC
(nucleotide excision repair). For row 18.

> [q8] The other two loci were not replicated. Rs1043943 in TMEM43 was nearly nominal significant (p = 0.057) in parents’ life span study by Timmers et al. but not significant in Deelen's study (p > 0.5).

The paper's own European look-up: not replicated; Timmers 2019 P 0.057, Deelen 2019 P > 0.5. For row 18.

> [q10] Except for the BRAF and BMPER loci, we did not observe any other loci that reached genome‐wide significance.

Liu's own Cox survival analysis of 12,664 in dataset 1 (3,040 deaths): only BRAF and BMPER genome-wide; rs1043943's
value is in Table S16, not read. For row 18.

> [q11] The TMEM43 locus was associated with Parkinson's disease, AST, and UA.

Parkinson's disease (P 0.045, one uncorrected test in dataset 1), and AST and uric acid in BioBank Japan; the
direction is not given in the text. For row 18.

> [q12] Next, a meta‐analysis was performed on the two case–control association results, using inverse‐variance weighted fixed‐effect meta‐analysis in METAL software (https://genome.sph.umich.edu/wiki/METAL).

The meta-analysis method: inverse-variance fixed effect (METAL). For row 18.

> [q13] It has been reported that rs1043943 might regulate the expression of XPC, which is a nucleotide excision repair (NER) gene involved in DNA damage repair, and the deletion of XPC leads to the development of lung tumors in mice (Hollander et al., 2005).

The paper's reading of the effector: XPC, a nucleotide-excision-repair gene. For row 18.
