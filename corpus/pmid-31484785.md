Wright et al. 2019, G3 (Bethesda). GWAS of parents' lifespan in AncestryDNA customers linked to public family
trees (paternal and maternal, birth cohorts 1886–1918 and 1886–1940; up to about 309,000 genotyped offspring for
fathers), then a METAL meta-analysis with UK Biobank parental attained-age results (about 658,000 to 698,000). The
AncestryDNA arm is the one sample in this literature outside UK Biobank and the LifeGen cohorts: mostly American
customers. The meta-analysis is not independent of Pilling 2017, Joshi 2017 or Timmers 2019, since its UK Biobank
half is the same biobank. Read from Europe PMC's open full text (PMC6723124); CC BY 4.0.

Table 3, paternal lifespan, LDLR: meta-analysis P 2.73 × 10⁻⁹; AncestryDNA P 6.78 × 10⁻⁵, effect 0.016 (SE 0.004);
UK Biobank P 1.70 × 10⁻⁷, effect 0.018 (SE 0.003). The two arms agree in sign. Table 3 prints no effect allele.

> [q2] The z-score transformed variant effect sizes and standard errors are listed for the AncestryDNA and UKB analyses.

Table 3's caption: its effects are z-scores, on no scale the format names (`effect_measure` is beta, HR or OR).

> [q3] We did not estimate the Cox proportional hazard of mortality, as in other analyses (Joshi et al. 2016; Pilling et al. 2017), because of the prevalence of missing year of death data in the SAP.

The AncestryDNA arm regresses parents' lifespan in years (40 to 120) on offspring dosage. It is not a Cox model.

> [q4] However, we suspect these loci to affect lifespan via increased risk for cardiovascular disease because they were previously associated with this disease (Nikpay et al. 2015; van der Harst and Verweij 2018) and it was the leading cause of death in the United States from 1980 to 2014 (Dwyer-Lindgren et al. 2016).

"These loci" are ANRIL, LPA, APOE, LDLR and LPL. The authors read them as acting through cardiovascular disease.

> [q5] If so, the signal of association would not be replicated in the comparison between the primarily-British vs. primarily-American cohorts.

The authors treat the AncestryDNA and UK Biobank arms as separate populations, which is what makes the AncestryDNA
arm a replication sample for the UK Biobank and LifeGen loci.

> [q6] In addition, Supplemental Tables 8 and 9 are provided as Excel documents and contain SNP-by-SNP summary statistics from each presented GWAS for representative SNPs with genomic control adjusted P values less than 3.0e-3 in the AncestryDNA cohorts (Supplemental Table 8) or 2.5e-3 in the AncestryDNA-UKB meta analyses (Supplemental Table 9).
